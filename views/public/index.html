What to report

You can report any of these

These are the incident types you will choose from in the form. If your situation does not fit neatly into one, choose Other / Complex and describe it in your own words.

Physical Bullying

Hitting, pushing, tripping, or damaging and taking someone's belongings.

Verbal Bullying

Name-calling, insults, threats, slurs, or repeated humiliating remarks.

Cyberbullying

Harassment through messages, group chats, or social media — including fake accounts and shared photos.

Social Exclusion

Deliberately isolating someone, spreading rumors, or turning classmates against them.

Other / Complex

Anything else that makes campus feel unsafe, or a situation that involves several of the above.

Not sure if it counts?

Report it anyway. Sorting that out is our job, not yours.

The process

What happens after you submit

Nothing about your report is posted publicly and no one is confronted without the Guidance Office reviewing the case first. You can follow the progress yourself using your tracking code — you never have to ask anyone in person.

About your tracking code

When you finish the form, the system gives you a code that looks like DG-4F9A2C. Save a screenshot of it. It is the only way to check your case, and because reports can be anonymous, a lost code cannot be recovered for you.

  1. 1

    You submit the report

    Describe what happened, when, and where. Names are optional. You may attach one photo or document as evidence.

  2. 2

    The case is logged as Pending

    Your report reaches the Guidance Office queue right away and waits for assessment of its category and severity.

  3. 3

    It moves to Under Investigation

    A designated officer gathers context, speaks with the people involved as needed, and records notes on the case.

  4. 4

    The case is Resolved or Escalated

    Action is taken under the school's anti-bullying policy. Serious cases are escalated to school administration. The outcome shows on your tracking page.

Your privacy

What we ask for, and what we do with it

Reporting should never put you at risk. Here is exactly how your information is handled.

Your name is optional

Leave the name and contact fields blank and your report is filed as an anonymous submission. Only the incident details are reviewed.

Only staff can open it

Reports are not visible to other students. Access requires a Guidance Office account, and every case is handled as a confidential school record.

Protected by law

The Anti-Bullying Act of 2013 (RA 10627) requires schools to keep reports confidential and forbids retaliation against anyone who files one.

Attaching evidence: you can upload one screenshot, photo, or document — JPG, PNG, or PDF, up to 5 MB. Blur or crop anything you are not comfortable sharing; evidence is helpful but never required.

Questions

Before you report

Will the person I report find out it was me?
Not from this system. Your identity is never shown to the person being reported. If you leave the name and contact fields blank, the Guidance Office does not have your name either — though giving it lets them follow up with you if they need more details.
What if I only saw it happen to someone else?
Please report it. Choose Student (Witness / Bystander) as your role. Many students who are being bullied cannot speak up for themselves, and a witness report is treated just as seriously.
How long does a review take?
The form is open at any hour, but reports are read during school days. Check your tracking code to see when the status moves from Pending to Under Investigation. If the situation gets worse while you wait, go to the Guidance Office in person.
I lost my tracking code. Can I get it back?
The code cannot be resent, because anonymous reports have no contact details attached to them. Your report is still in the system and will still be reviewed — you just will not be able to track it online. Visit the Guidance Office if you need an update.
What if I am not certain about the details?
Write what you do remember and say plainly which parts you are unsure about. An honest partial account is useful. Filing a knowingly false report, however, is a separate offense under the student handbook.

Bullying stops when someone tells.

It takes about five minutes, your name is optional, and nothing happens to you for speaking up.
